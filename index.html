HARDWARE

Web-Shooters & Gadgets

Built web-shooters, mini gadgets, and DIY contraptions from YouTube tutorials and scrap parts. The Iron Man obsession started here.

This was the moment my imagination finally escaped the paper and took over my bedroom floor. 🛠️ Growing up, I was basically a full time daydreamer. I drew constantly, filling pages with ideas that probably made perfect sense to me and absolutely nobody else. Then I got my hands on some actual building materials (yes, those legendary alloy building blocks), and everything changed. 🔄 I stopped thinking only in drawings and started wondering how I could turn those ideas into something real. Armed with YouTube tutorials, random bits of scrap, and probably more confidence than actual knowledge, I turned my free time into a full time mad scientist operation. If I could imagine it, I had to try building it. Whether it worked was a completely separate matter. 😭 Somewhere along the way, I discovered Marvel, fell in love with it, and decided that apparently being a normal kid was not enough😁. I needed gadgets, inventions, and my own little version of Tony Stark's workshop. Looking back, these projects were messy, impractical, and occasionally questionable from a safety perspective. But they were also the beginning of something real: my love for creating things instead of just imagining them. HTML. CSS. Basic Python. Self-taught, no tutor. The first website was terrible. Shipped it anyway.

2024
CREATIVE

3D Animation & Design

Blender. Unreal Engine 5. Premiere Pro. Started an instagram channel. Made things look cinematic.

Finding out that you could build entire 3D worlds using free software absolutely blew my mind🤯. Suddenly, I could bring my ideas into 3D, give them lighting, add movement, and make them feel like something straight out of a movie,(like one of those MARVEL's😁). Naturally, I went down the rabbit hole. I taught myself Blender from scratch, explored Unreal Engine 5, learned video editing, and started creating things just to see how far I could take them. Before I knew it, 3D design had become as natural to me as drawing once was. The only problem? Render times🫩. I can confidently say Blender has probably claimed more hours of my life than any other software. But this phase wasn't just about learning software. It completely changed how I saw art and design. I started paying attention to lighting, composition, colours, atmosphere, and the little details that make something feel right. I was learning how to create art, but also how to recognise good art. Looking back, this was my own little taste curation renaissance, a phrase I didn't even know existed back then. 😅 More than anything, this era taught me that creativity isn't just about having ideas. It's about learning how to present them, how to make people feel something, and how to turn an ordinary scene into something worth looking at twice. 2025
AI

JARVIS Experiments

Local AI assistants with Ollama. Automation with n8n. The dream of building a real JARVIS — still alive.

The first time I got my hands on ChatGPT, something changed. I went from using the internet to find answers to having entire conversations with a machine. Naturally, I became a relentless chatterbox😂. Suddenly, reading, writing, and exploring ideas didn't feel like chores anymore. I actually wanted to do them. At first, AI was more of a place to ask questions and explore random thoughts. But as I learned more, it became a genuine co-pilot for untangling complicated ideas, testing my thinking, and exploring things I might never have figured out on my own. The more I understood what AI could do, the more I started wondering about something bigger. What if I could build my own? That question eventually turned into my personal JARVIS project🦾. I started imagining an assistant that could understand context, remember things, manage tasks, automate workflows, and work alongside me rather than just sit there waiting for a prompt. Ollama introduced me to running AI models locally, while n8n opened up a whole new world of automation. Little by little, the idea grew into an entire ecosystem of connected features. Of course, imagining an intelligent assistant is considerably easier than building one. 😭 My current setup is nowhere near the fictional JARVIS, and I still have plenty to learn. But I've been documenting the architecture, planning features, and figuring out how the different pieces could work together. The goal is to turn that growing blueprint into something real when I have the resources and skills to do it justice. And one small clarification before anyone starts calling me an Iron Man wannabe. 😂 JARVIS is just a temporary name I'm using throughout my notes to explain the idea. I'll definitely choose an original name for the actual project🗿😎. Honestly, even Ultron had a more mature naming strategy😭. If the documentation is uploaded by the time you're reading this, you'll be able to explore the project below. It's still a work in progress, but the dream is very much alive 🚀.
